The owner of a small cabin in Oravská Poruba, a village in the north of Slovakia, didn't want his own face anywhere near his brand, so he offered me his dog's face instead, which to him felt like the safer option.

Her name is Stela, she is a border collie, and today she is officially the owner of Stelin Home.

## A dog sounded like a limit

My first reaction was not excitement. A brand built around one specific animal felt risky to me, because it would decide the look of everything that came after, and I was worried it would limit where the brand could go.

The longer I thought about it, the more the limit started to look like the point. A dog on the logo tells you, before you read a single word, that the cabin is pet friendly, and that speaks straight to the people most likely to book it: dog owners looking for a place where their dog is welcome too. The owner had given me a niche without calling it one, and my job was to make it visible.

## Finding Stela's face

We started by trying different variants of a dog right away, but in the end we went back to Stela's real face and built the character from it, so the logo shows one specific border collie with her own markings and a calm, friendly expression.

Along the way I kept showing the drafts to people outside the project and asking what they felt first when they saw them. Those first impressions helped me tune the details until the dog read as peaceful and kind rather than like a cartoon mascot.

The warmth of the brand comes from that calm dog and from the colours around her. The palette sits in olive green, cream and dark wood tones, which match the cabin itself, and the headlines use a classic serif.

## Stela runs the place

Once the logo existed, Stela started appearing everywhere. On the website she has her own story with a real photo and is introduced as the owner of the cabin, and on Instagram the account is called Stelkina Chatka, which means Stela's little cabin. My favourite post is the Halloween one, where she sits in front of the door under a white sheet, dressed as a ghost.

The brand also had to work for the owner on an ordinary Tuesday. He needed a simple way to take bookings and manage requests, so the website got its own booking system, and guests can book a stay directly instead of going through a big platform. For Instagram I made a set of templates with labels like holidays, atmosphere and booking, and he fills them with his own photos and posts them himself. The account has grown to around 340 followers without paid ads, and some of the guests who came brought their own dogs along, which is exactly who the brand was made for.

## What I'd change

If I started again, I would spend more time on a reversed version of the logo. On dark backgrounds, like the dark mode on a phone, Stela doesn't look as good as she does on cream, and a brand that people mostly see on their phones should feel at home on both.

The bigger lesson is about the idea I first saw as a risk. What looked like a limit at the start, one specific dog on every piece of the brand, turned out to be the clearest way to tell the right guests that this place is for them and their dogs.

Has a client ever handed you a constraint that ended up being the best part of the project?
