Put the logos of ten bike rentals next to each other and you'll mostly see a wheel, a gear or a bike drawn from the side, and around the lakes of Orava it's no different. Those logos tell you what the place rents, but none of them says anything about how the ride will feel.

That's the category I stepped into when Tilia Resort asked me to create Bicykľáreň, a bike rental under its own brand, which became my biggest freelance project so far and the first one I led from the first brainstorm to launch.

## Two concepts and a blank page

The brief had a tension in it, because the rental had to feel like part of the resort and still have a personality of its own, and it also had to feel traditional and family friendly, like the resort itself. I kept the resort's colours and typeface, so the connection was clear from the start.

I usually present three logo concepts. I had two ready, and after a briefing with the client I knew the third one had to be completely different from both of them, but I was blocked and couldn't come up with anything.

## Drawing the ride

So I went deeper and looked for a gap, something no other rental was doing. Every logo in the category showed the bike, and none of them showed what people actually come for, which is the afternoon on the trail, a route that keeps going without stops.

That became the third concept. The wordmark is written in one continuous line, like a route you ride from start to finish without stopping, and it also had the traditional, family-friendly feel the brief asked for. I presented it next to the other two concepts, and it won.

I used the same rule for the illustrations, so every drawing in the brand looks like part of one smooth trip, and the line travelled onto everything a guest touches, from the stickers on the bikes and the shop window to flags, posters, flyers and a cap.

## Keeping the promise

A brand built around an easy ride makes a promise, and the booking had to keep it, so before I designed the reservation flow I needed to understand how the rental works on the ground: who hands out the bikes, at what time and what can go wrong. Only then did I design the steps from choosing a bike and dates to confirming the booking.

The reviews suggest it worked. One customer wrote about "easy ordering using the QR code, friendly staff", and another said the bikes were in perfect condition and that in two hours they saw more than they would have on foot in a whole day. People rarely mention a booking flow unless it gets in their way, so I read the first review as a good sign.

## What I'd take to the next project

Looking back, the best idea came only after I got stuck and started looking at what everyone else in the category was doing, so I could find the place where nobody was. The question that moved me forward was what customers actually come for, and the answer was a feeling.

I'd also watch two risks next time. A hidden meaning stays hidden if nobody tells it, so the story of the line needs a place on the website and in posts, and a handwritten wordmark has to stay readable on a small sticker or a phone screen, which is worth testing early.
